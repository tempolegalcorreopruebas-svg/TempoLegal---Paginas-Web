Data management

External or internal DPO?

Roles and considerations when organizing data protection support.

Tempo LegalBusiness edition2 min read

In this reading

  2. 02Define the function
  3. 03Two models to assess
  4. 04Compare scope and deliverables
  5. 05Review the proposal
← Back to insights

01

Look beyond the budget

Choosing an internal or external DPO involves independence, operational knowledge and continuity. Before comparing fees, establish whether your organization must designate a DPO and which processing activities need the most attention.

Map the entities, locations, processes, suppliers and data categories involved. This context makes proposals comparable and helps avoid a function that exists only in an appointment document.

02

Define the function

The DPO advises, monitors and cooperates with the authority. Responsibility for business decisions and implementation does not automatically transfer to the officer. Distinguishing these roles helps prevent conflicts of interest.

Set out reporting lines, access to documentation and a route for escalating risks. The role requires access to operations and effective communication with senior management.

03

Two models to assess

An internal professional may know the business well and be available daily. Assess qualifications, workload, autonomy and possible incompatibilities with other positions.

An external service may provide specialist knowledge and contractual continuity. It still needs an understanding of actual operations, regular meetings, access to information and replacement arrangements. Neither model alone guarantees independent or sufficient oversight.

04

Compare scope and deliverables

Assess cost against time commitment, the number of entities, data complexity and included activities. Separate oversight, implementation, legal representation and technical support; these are not necessarily one service.

A practical plan can include an initial assessment, priorities, periodic meetings and progress reports. Such a calendar is an operational tool to adapt to the organization, rather than a uniform list of legally mandated monthly tasks.

05

Review the proposal

Ask about the designated professional, independence, availability, confidential information and how recommendations are documented. Review termination arrangements and the handover of records.

The final question is whether the organization can demonstrate an active function with adequate information, risk follow-up and access to management when a decision requires it.

Reference framework

  • Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales, arts. 48–50
  • Reglamento General a la LOPDP, arts. 48–57
  • Resolución SPDP-SPD-2025-0028-R
